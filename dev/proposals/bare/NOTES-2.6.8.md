# Notes for 2.6.8: passwords and PINs in meeting invites

**Fixed in 2.6.8 (a client leak that is also in 2.6.7).** A meeting ID or passcode written between underscores (`__Passcode:__ 4417`, `_Passcode_: 4417`, `Meeting_ID: 987 654 321`, `Passcode_4417`) was kept in a saved calendar title, place or invite text, and from there reached the Prep research request, the agenda and follow-up drafts and the minutes of that meeting. The same held for several other forms: more than two blank lines after the label, a label followed by a dash or an arrow instead of a colon, several labels on one line, "Zoom ID 4417", "Room ID - 4417", "Meeting: 987 654 321", "ID is 4417", "Pass-code: 4417" and "Token! 4417". All are now removed. Old saved entries are cleaned again when a session starts and before every reader (Today, Prep, drafts, Close the day, transcripts) uses them.

**Fixed.** A meeting title, place, organiser or invite text that says "Password", "PIN", "code", "token", "secret", "OTP", "API key", "Pw" or "Pswd" (also "P I N", "P.I.N.") followed by a plain word, for example "Password swordfish", now has that word treated as the secret. The whole title, place or organiser is dropped (a title becomes "Meeting"), or the line is dropped from the invite text. A calendar saved by an earlier version is cleaned again whenever it is read, and rewritten clean when a session starts.

**You may notice (on purpose).** To be safe, after one of those labels every word up to the end must belong to a short list of ordinary phrases, otherwise the first other word counts as the secret. These titles now show as "Meeting": Pin the roadmap, Password for all staff, Code sprint, Code clinic, Code Red, Pin board, Secret Santa party, Secret sauce, Token of appreciation, Secret project Falcon, Password reset for new hires. These phrases stay (alone, or followed only by words such as for, and, the, request, plan, workshop, session, review, sync): Password reset, policy, manager, management, expiry, rotation, audit, change; PIN entry, PIN pad; Code review, reviews, of conduct, base, freeze, quality, repository, handover, walkthrough, standards; Token refresh, expiry, rotation, economy, economics, Ring; Secret santa, management, manager, rotation; OTP setup, rollout, enrolment; API key rotation, management, review. A colon, equals sign, dash or bracket after the label or after one of those words always means a secret: "Password reset: hunter", "PIN entry swordfish" and "Password reset for hunter" are removed.

**The lines after a label.** What happens depends on how the label line ends.
- A label alone on a line, or ending on a separator or a function word ("Passcode:", "Password reset for", "Password reset -"): the next lines are its value. The next three lines with text go, whatever they say (blank lines do not count), and the label line goes with them. A label list ("Meeting ID:", "Passcode:") removes one value line for each label.
- A finished phrase that leads its line ("Password reset", "Password policy", "Code review", "Code freeze", "Code of conduct", "Dress code", "Secret Santa", "Token refresh"; Alan's option B, bare19): only the lines among the next three that HOLD A NUMBER OR AN ID go: four or more digits, digits in groups (987 654 321), a word that mixes letters and digits ("hunter2", "ZQ7742"; not everyday codes such as Q3, H2, 3D, 2nd, 10am, v2.1) or a long hex-like string; a list marker (1., a), i), -, a bullet, an en dash, `<li>`) is stripped first and the rest judged the same way. A word-only line, a sentence and a line of plain words always stay, so "Code review" followed by "Training dates / Sign-off / Reporting line / AOB" (numbered or not) loses nothing. The heading goes only if one of those three lines was removed. "IT update: password reset" opens nothing. **Hard labels** (Passcode, PIN, Password, Access code, Meeting ID, Host key and their provider, glued, hyphenated and bulleted forms) keep the full rule: the next lines go whatever they hold, a list item included, and an instruction between ("Please enter") does not use the label up (two weak lines at most).
- "Meeting ID." (a stop mark) takes the next line with a number; a removed line (a link, a passcode line) in between does not use it up.

**What it still over-removes, and what it now leaks (the lines rule).**
- Over-removes: a label word followed by an ordinary word that is not on the lists ("Dress code for visitors", "Cost code changes", "Pin board", "Token budget") loses its own line; so do the titles in the reviewer's agenda measurement (below). The old over-removal of one- and two-word lines after a soft heading is gone (option B).
- Leaks (known limits, pinned by tests): **a word-only secret typed right under a soft heading stays: "Password reset" then "hunter" (also as `- hunter`, `1. hunter`, `<li>hunter</li>`)** (`SOFT_LIMITS`, `test_the_known_limit_a_word_only_secret_under_a_soft_heading`); a value of three plain words on a line of its own ("correct horse battery"); a value more than three lines below the heading; a word-only value after "Meeting ID" with no separator. A word-only value under a hard label (Passcode:, PIN, Meeting ID:) does go, also as a list item.

**Labels in a row.** When several labels stand alone one after another ("Meeting ID:", "Passcode:", "PIN:"), the next lines with text are removed one for each label, so a label list followed by its values loses exactly the values; if a label has no value of its own, one ordinary line after the list is lost with them. A / or | straight after a label ("API key / ...") now counts as a separator like a dash.

**Separators.** After a label, any punctuation or symbol (not just a colon) means a secret follows: "Passcode –", "Passcode →", "Passcode is", "Passcode (", also "Key:" on its own line. The line after goes with it. This also removes titles such as "Access & Security review" or "Key (draft): plan" that have a label word followed by a symbol; a sentence end (full stop, comma, semicolon, ! or ?) does not count. "Key milestones" and "Key dates" are unaffected.

**Sentence ends (a deliberate trade-off).** A separator with full stops, commas, question or exclamation marks round it ("Password.: hunter", "Passcode:!", "PIN?:") and an ellipsis ("Passcode...", "Passcode…") count as a separator. Two or more sentence-end marks in a row count as an ellipsis. A label followed only by ONE sentence end and then a plain word is kept on purpose, because in ordinary prose a sentence ends there and a new one starts: "Password! hunter" on one line, and a line "Passcode." followed by a line "hunter" (the label line goes, the next stays). A label followed by a sentence end and then a number ("Meeting ID. 123 456", "Meeting ID, 123", "Access code. 4417") is removed.

**Underscores and markdown.** Underscores, asterisks, tildes and backticks round a label are read as spaces, so `snake_case` words are read as words: "pass_code", "meeting_id 4417" and "api_key abc" are removed, "code_review" and "my_password_manager plan" stay. A label line followed by a removed line (a link, a line with its own value) loses the next ordinary line as well, because the removed line is not taken for the value.

**Accents.** An accent or other combining mark on a label letter no longer hides the label ("Pásscode: 1234", "Piń: 1234", "Passwörd hunter", "Sécret"). Words that differ from a label only by accents are read as the label, so "Códe sprint", "Piñ board" and "Tokén of appreciation" lose their text to "Meeting" like their plain forms; "Códe review" and "Piñ pad" stay, and "Passwörter" is a different word and stays. The text that is kept keeps its accents. A markdown link whose text is a label ("[Passcode](http://...)") is read as that label.

**Odd characters.** A character that would turn into more than four characters when normalised (a ligature such as U+FDFA, which stands for eighteen letters, or a unit sign) is read as a space before it is expanded, so a hostile invite cannot make reading slow. No label letter expands that far.

**Saved calendars.** A saved meeting keeps only the fields the sweep writes (title, place, times, organiser, invite text, attendees, attachment names and a few flags); anything else in an old or foreign file (a description, a join link, a note at the top, a row that is not a meeting) is dropped when the file is cleaned and when it is read. The clean-up and a sweep's save take the same lock, so neither loses the other's write. A cleaned calendar is kept in memory while the file is unchanged, so repeated reads are fast.

**Details for the release note (Romanoff).** The saved-calendar clean-up always runs: if the calendar lock cannot be had within a few seconds (another process holds it, the lock file cannot be written, or the system has no file locking) it cleans without the lock and writes only if the file has not changed since it was read, and says so in the debug log. The read-side cache is keyed on modified time, size and inode; a rewrite that restores all three would be served the earlier CLEANED content, which is stale but never a leak. Day keys that are not YYYY-MM-DD dates and extra fields on attendees are dropped by design. The daily backup zips and their OneDrive copies keep an earlier raw `calendar.json` for their retention period.

**Idempotent cleaning.** Cleaning a saved text twice gives the same text as cleaning it once (a 40,000-body seeded test and Team Cap's 35-test property file). A kept label line whose window removed a line goes with it, so a later pass cannot open it again, and the window counts every line, so removals do not shift later lines into it. Two of Team Cap's tests ask that the one- and two-word lines after a leading heading survive; they are marked as accepted limits in `test_calendar_clean_idempotence.py` because such a line looks exactly like a value ("hunter").

## bare20: glued labels for every hard label, "Key" in list items, the exemption rule (reviewer's rerun on cc67a8d6)

**Glued labels, one general rule.** For every hard label (Token, Secret, OTP, API key, Meeting / Zoom / Webinar / Conference ID, Host key, Access code, Pass code, Passcode, Password, Pwd, PIN, and their provider-prefixed forms) a label written straight against a value, with no separator, is a value and the field or line goes. The value is three or more digits ("Token4417", "API key987 654 321"), or a token that mixes letters and digits with at least one digit ("PINZQ7742", "PINQ3A9", "Meeting IDZQ7742", "OTPH2K7", "Zoom IDH2K7"). A plain word glued on is just a longer word and stays. The regex is `GLUED_VALUE` in `invite_clean.py`. Negative rows (kept): ID badges, Idaho 1234, Pinterest 2026, Tokenise, Secretary 2026, Pinot, Pinot Noir 2026, Optical, Secretariat, Keyboard shortcuts, Keynote, Passport control, Passover, Pineapple, Codec review, Accessible design, Token4 and Key4 (one digit, no letters). Fail-closed cost: an ordinary word glued to a label with a digit ("Secretary2026") goes. A bare "ID" glued to letters and digits ("Idaho1234") is not a label glued rule (only "ID" + 3 digits is), so it stays.

**"Key" alone in a list item or table cell.** "- Key", "* Key", "1. Key", "a) Key", "> Key", "| Key |" and `<li>Key</li>` are a hard label: the next lines go whatever they hold ("- ZQ7742", "> ZQ7742", "hunter"). "Key milestones", "Key dates", "Key risks" stay. Known limits that stay: a word-only value after a bare "ID" label ("Meeting ID hunterxyz"), and an unmarked "Key" line followed by "- hunter".

**The everyday-code exemption, exact rule.** Under a SOFT heading ("Password reset", "Code review", "Dress code") a line is removed if it holds a number as `id_number` reads it, a strong token, or a mixed token (`_mixed`). `_mixed(w)` is: the token has a letter and a digit AND `_CODE_LIKE.fullmatch(w)` is false. `_CODE_LIKE` is a FULL match of one of: one or two letters + one or two digits (Q3, H2, A4), one or two digits + one or two letters (3D), a digit run + st/nd/rd/th/am/pm (2nd, 10am), or v + digits with dots (v2.1). So Q3, H2, 3D, 2nd, 10am, v2.1 stay; Q3A9, H2K7, A1B2 (two codes glued) do not fully match and go. Rows and a prefix-match mutant are in `test_invite_clean_bare20.py`.

**Known limit (coordinator).** A bare two-character letter+digit passcode such as "X9" has the same shape as "Q3": under a soft heading it is kept (row pinned in the tests as a limit).

**Unreadable calendar reply.** An unreadable calendar reply is discarded (there is nothing to retry from): the raw draft never stays in staging and never reaches the backup zip (Cap #378, pinned by `test_v268_calendar_draft_leak`).

**Fuzz.** 70,000 list-heavy bodies (Key items, glued labels, bullets, numbered, quotes, cells, `<li>`) plus 30,000 from the older list generator and the existing 40,000 + 40,000: 0 non-fixed-points, 0 values after pass one.

## bare21: qualified hard labels, dates and times under a soft heading (Team Cap's verdict on bare19)

**Qualified hard label (HIGH, also in 2.6.7).** `Passcode for dial-in:` then `4417` left `4417`; so did `Meeting ID (Teams)` then `987 654 321`. Rule in plain words: a hard label (Passcode, PIN, Password, Access code, Meeting ID, Host key and their provider forms) followed by a qualifier and then a separator or the end of the line stands alone, exactly like an unqualified label: the next lines are its value (one line for each label in a row; an instruction such as "Please enter" does not use the label up; blank lines do not count; list markers, `<li>` and table cells are read the same). A qualifier is either a bracketed group (`(Teams)`, `(organiser only)`; a footnote mark `(1)` is the older footnote rule) or a FUNCTION WORD (for, to, of, on, at, in, via, by, from, when, if, used, only, per, during, with, as) and at most four more plain words (`for dial-in`, `to join`, `for the weekly call`), or both. A word with a digit is never part of a qualifier, so `Passcode for dial-in 4417` is a same-line value, as before. Regexes: `_QFUNC`, `_QUAL`, `QUALIFIED_ALONE` in `invite_clean.py`, joined into `LABEL_ALONE`. Rows: `test_invite_clean_bare21.py` (21 labels x 9 values x 11 layouts = 2,079 rows, plus same-line, two-in-a-row, idempotence).

**Negatives (behave as today).** The word after the label must be a function word or a bracket. `Passcode policy review`, `Password reset rollout plan`, `Meeting ID badges`, `Pin the roadmap`, `Code of conduct refresh`, `Access review for the quarter` open nothing: the label lines that were removed before are still removed, and the next lines stay. The value after a qualified label is bounded like an unqualified one: one line per label (plus up to two instruction lines), so a fourth plain line stays.

**Dates and times under a soft heading (MEDIUM).** `Code freeze` then `12-11-2026` lost the date. Now a plausible date or time under a SOFT heading (Password reset, Code review, Dress code, Token refresh, Code freeze) is an agenda line and stays: 12-11-2026, 2026-11-12, 12/11/2026, 12.11.2026 (day first, year first, or month first; month 1-12, day 1-31, year 19xx/20xx or two digits), 12 Nov 2026, 12 November 2026, 12th November 2026, Nov 12, November 12, 2026, Fri 12 Nov, 12:30, 09:00-17:30, 12:30-13:00 (hyphen or en dash), 9am-5pm, 9:30 am, 14 Nov 2026 14:00. The date or time is taken out of the line and what is left is judged as before, so `12 Nov 4417`, `14:00 4417`, `Nov 12 hunter2` and `987-65-4321` (not a valid date) still go; 4417, 987 654 321 and ZQ7742 under a soft heading still go. A line of dates alone never takes its heading with it. `_SOFT_DATE_TIME` and `_looks_like_value(line, dates_ok=True)`; only the soft window passes `dates_ok`.

**Hard labels keep dates as values.** After `Passcode:`, `PIN`, `Meeting ID:`, a qualified label and the rest, a date or time goes like any other line (`_weak_phrase` does not use the exemption, so a date is never mistaken for an instruction line). A date-shaped passcode on the label line (`Passcode: 12-11-2026`) goes.

**New costs and limits.**
- A line that ends on a hard label and a function word phrase ("Please share the Password for the portal", "Passcode for staff") now removes the next line with text as its value (fail closed). A one-sentence ending in "... Password for X" followed by an agenda line loses that line.
- A date or time typed as a passcode right under a SOFT heading ("Password reset" then "12-11-2026", "Password reset" then "14:00") stays. It cannot be told from a deadline; hard labels do not have this limit.
- Titles, places and organisers are unchanged: a title "Meeting ID for the weekly call" with no value is kept (a title has no next line).
- FIXED in bare21 (digit leak): `id_number` (the `Meeting ID.` / `Key,` stop-label rule and the soft test) took out dates with a loose pattern, so 987-65-4321, 123-45-6789 and 1234-56-7890 after a hard label were kept. It now takes out only VALID dates (day 1-31, month 1-12, year 19xx/20xx or two digits) and times; those number shapes now go after every hard label. A real date or time right after a stop label ("Bring your staff ID." then 25-10-2026 or 12:30) is still kept, as before (needed so "Lunch at 12:30" survives); after a colon-style hard label (Passcode:, PIN, qualified labels) a real date goes. Rows and a mutant in `test_invite_clean_bare21.py` (`StopLabelNumbers`).
- A qualifier longer than five words, a separator before the qualifier ("Password - for guests": the line goes, its next line stays), a qualifier that starts with a plain word ("Passcode dial-in:" is the older unqualified form; "Passcode policy review" is a phrase), and a qualifier in another alphabet are not covered.

**Fuzz (all seeded).** 40,000 plain bodies, 40,000 list bodies, 70,000 list-heavy bodies (and the 30,000 older-generator list bodies), and a NEW 40,000 body generator mixing dates, times, qualified labels, soft headings, negatives, list markers and blank lines: 0 non-fixed-points, 0 surviving values. A separate 8,000-body run of value-free bodies loses no date or time. Mutants (killed): qualified rule off, no bracketed group, no function-word qualifier, qualifier of 0 or 3 extra words, qualifier that takes any word (eats "Passcode policy review"), qualifier with no end anchor, dates not exempt, numeric dates, month names and times each off, an exemption that takes every number, invalid dates accepted, and the weak-phrase path exempting dates.
