## 1. The alert sweep looks beyond the people you follow (future)
**Idea (Alan):** expand the scope of the alert sweep (today's alert check, which watches only messages from the people you follow) so it can look beyond them.
**Starting points, not decided:** what else is worth an alert (for example a message that mentions a project, a milestone, a deadline or one of your own items; mail from a client domain; a reply to something you chased); how it would be shaped by the same rules as today's alerts (no VIP wording or mark anywhere, BL-288; confidential content left out; counts and references only in the record); the cost, which rises with scope (see #97: the check costs about $4.80 a day for the followed people alone, so a wider scope needs the cost levers first); and how Today shows it (the Messages section and Needs attention).
**Before design:** Alan says what he wants alerted that he is not alerted on today; the Usage tab's real cost data; Jarvis checks the unattended run and the read-only tool list.

## 2. "Talk to the page": shape Today's sections by talking to ask creaite
**Idea (Alan):** use ask creaite to shape the rules for the sections on Today, just as he does today to include or exclude meetings from the header ("don't show the weekly sync", "show only meetings with a client"). The plan is to extend this so he can shape what is shown or hidden in each separate section: Needs attention, Your commitments, Week ahead, Messages, Portfolio.
**Starting points, not decided:** a small set of per-section rules kept as settings (for example hide items from a project, hide a kind, show only the next three days, hide meetings with certain words), each readable and changeable in Settings as well as by asking; ask creaite proposes the rule in words and the exact setting change on the approval card, and it is undoable; it is a change to a display setting, never to the record; rules are per person and the same on every machine (kept in settings.json); the header's meeting include/exclude rules are the model and become the first of the sections.
**Before design:** Alan lists the first three rules he would want; Jarvis checks how today's header rules are stored and whether the settings route (guarded) can carry per-section rules; a mockup of how a section shows that a rule is hiding things ("2 hidden") so nothing is lost silently.
