# The executive voice

How every creaite status sounds: the Current state, the weekly report, the client report, the steering one-pager and the draft email. The reader is senior, has thirty seconds, and wants to know what to do. Write the position, the reason, what it means and what is needed.

## The shape

1. **Headline (one or two sentences, in bold).** The position in a word the reader knows (on track, at risk, off track) and the one thing that matters most, with the date or number. Example: "**On track for the 20-11-2026 go-live; the one thing to watch is UAT data, due from the client by 16-10-2026.**"
2. **Why (two or three points).** Each point is a fact and its "so what": what changed since the last status, progress against plan or target, and what it means for the date, cost or outcome. In a short status these are bullets that begin with a bold lead-in (**Why.**).
3. **Risks that matter (at most two).** What could happen, how likely, what is being done, who owns it. Never a list of every risk.
4. **Asks and decisions.** Each says who, by when, and what happens if it waits. Example: "We need Sara Khan to confirm the pilot extension by 16-10-2026; later than 23-10-2026 moves go-live a week."
5. **Next period (two or three).** Outcomes to be reached, not tasks to be done.

Leave out a part that has nothing to say. Say "Nothing has changed since the last status" when that is the news. Never pad.

## The rules

- **Bottom line first.** The first two sentences carry everything a reader who stops there needs.
- **Say what matters and drop the rest.** Ten things happened and two matter: write the two. A page is the norm; cut a draft by a third before sending it.
- **Quantify.** Dates, percentages and comparisons with target or plan, not adjectives. "Two days late" beats "slightly delayed".
- **Names and dates.** "Sara Khan by 16-10-2026", not "the team soon".
- **Outcomes before activities.** "UAT signed off" is an outcome; "UAT meetings held" is an activity.
- **No tracker language.** No item IDs (ABC-A-003), no "action", "RAID" or "register" in the prose. The sources are listed after the draft, not inside it.
- **Plain words.** Short sentences. No filler, no hedging ("it seems", "may potentially"), no jargon the reader doesn't use.
- **Never claim what the material doesn't support.** If the facts are thin, say so in one short sentence.
- British English; dates as DD-MM-YYYY; the formats the editor allows only (bold, italic, "- " bullets, "### " sub-headings, quotes, links; no tables).

## Audience

- **Internal:** candid. Say what is wrong and who owns it. Use the internal view of every item.
- **Client-safe:** the same shape, plainer, with no internal remarks. Only items marked client-visible, in their client wording. Nothing about internal disagreement, cost to us, staffing or people's performance.

## Length

- **Short:** the headline and one line, about 40 words.
- **Standard:** the shape above in 120 to 150 words.
- **Fuller:** up to about 250 words; still headline first, still no more than two risks.

## Example (fictional)

**The item-by-item draft this replaces:**

> ABC-A-004 (UAT test data) is overdue by 6 days and is owned by Priya Nair. ABC-A-007 (security review sign-off) is due 14-10. ABC-R-002 (data quality) is open with High priority and ABC-R-005 (resource availability) is open with Medium priority. Minutes of 06-10 record that the pilot ran for two weeks. ABC-D-003 (extend pilot) was made on 02-10. Handling time was discussed.

**The executive version:**

> **At risk for the 20-11-2026 go-live: UAT data is six days late, and without it testing cannot start.**
>
> - **Why.** The two-week pilot finished with handling time down 11% against an 8% target, so the case for go-live holds. UAT data was due from the client on 03-10-2026 and has not arrived; every further week moves go-live a week. Security sign-off is on track for 14-10-2026.
> - **Risk.** Data quality in the client's extract is the main exposure: if it fails validation, testing restarts. Priya Nair is checking the first file on 12-10-2026.
> - **Ask.** We need Sara Khan to release the UAT data by 16-10-2026; after 23-10-2026 go-live moves to 27-11-2026.
> - **Next.** UAT starts by 19-10-2026; security sign-off by 14-10-2026.

## Forms (BL-227)

The same facts, written in the form the reader needs. The Current state follows the shape above; each other form keeps its own length. Only the forms read this section (report.style_guide leaves it out of the Current state's guide).

### Audiences for the forms
- **Steering committee:** internal wording, decisions and asks first, then the position; nothing marked confidential, restricted or Client-safe: no.
- **Sponsor:** internal wording, the position and what is needed from the sponsor first; nothing marked confidential, restricted or Client-safe: no.

### Steering note
A short note for a steering committee: a bold headline, then **Decisions needed** and **Asks** first, then **Why** and **Next**, as "- " bullets with a bold lead-in. Keep to the length chosen.

### Three-line update
Exactly three lines, one sentence each: the position, the reason, what happens next (or the ask). No headline, no bullets, at most 60 words in all.

### Email paragraph
One paragraph of plain prose for the body of an email, at most 120 words: position first, then the reason, then the ask with who and by when. No bullets, no bold.

### Teams message
One to three short sentences, at most 50 words, conversational but exact: the position and the one thing that matters. No headings or bullets.
